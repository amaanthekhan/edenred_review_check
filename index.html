Proposal: tell people where their money is

The highest-stakes case in the reviews is a deduction followed by silence. The smallest fix is proactive status messages on the channels the company already uses, which also work when the app itself won't open.

Also noticed, but a separate engineering problem. The largest single complaint is the app not opening, login errors and slowness (about 27% of negative reviews), concentrated in the v13 era. Reviewers also mention launch video ads, a "screen sharing detected" block on Android 16, and the removal of the right to change their PIN. A status message does not fix any of that. I'm flagging it because it is probably the biggest driver of the rating drop, and I can't judge its cause from outside.

Requirements (draft)

Written so a product, design and engineering team could estimate it. Every assumption is labelled.

Problem and goal

Problem. When money is deducted and something fails (a transfer, an ATM withdrawal, a card payment), the user has no visible status, no reference and no self-serve way to track or dispute it. Reviewers describe being sent to a single message channel and then hearing nothing.

Goal. Reduce repeat contacts and time-to-resolution for failed-after-debit cases without adding a new channel.

User stories
  • As a cardholder whose transfer or ATM withdrawal failed, I want to see its state (failed, reversal started, refunded) and an expected date, so I know my money is not lost.
  • As a cardholder, I want a reference number I can quote to support, so I don't have to explain from scratch.
  • As a cardholder who can't open the app, I want the same updates by SMS or WhatsApp, so I'm not locked out of information about my own money.
  • As a support agent, I want the failure and its state pre-attached to the conversation, so I can resolve in one contact.
Acceptance criteria
  • A transaction that fails after debit creates a case with a reference ID within a window to be set with Engineering.
  • The user receives a status message at: case opened, reversal initiated, funds returned, each in the user's app language.
  • Every message states the next step and an expected date range. No message asks the user to start a conversation by sending a greeting.
  • If the expected date passes with no reversal, the case escalates to a human agent automatically.
  • Messages never contain full card numbers, balances or ID details, and never contain links that ask for credentials.
Edge cases
  • Transfer to a wrong beneficiary is not a technical failure: route to a different path, since a reversal may not be possible.
  • Partial failures, duplicate debits, and failures that straddle an app update.
  • User has no data connection, a new phone number, or a shared phone.
  • Low-literacy users: short sentences, icons, and voice notes where the channel allows.
Success metrics
  • Primary: repeat contacts per failed transaction; median time from failure to refund.
  • Secondary: first-contact resolution rate; share of failure cases that reach a human only by escalation; share of 1 and 2 star reviews mentioning support or deductions.
  • Guardrails: message delivery failures; fraud or phishing reports that reference status messages.
Dependencies and assumptions (unverified)
  • Needs transaction-state data from the processing and issuing partners. I don't know its availability or latency.
  • Needs the messaging channel to allow outbound template messages under the provider's rules.
  • Day-one data request: failure rates by type, refund times, contact volume per failure, and how many cases currently resolve in one contact.
Rollout
  • Phase 1: status messages for failed international transfers only, A/B tested against current handling.
  • Phase 2: extend to ATM disputes and card-payment failures.
  • Phase 3: add in-app case status, for users who can open the app.
Risks
  • Status messages become a phishing template: verified sender, no credential links, and an in-app warning.
  • Over-promising dates: show ranges until reversal timing is measured.
  • Regulatory expectations on complaint handling and disclosure: check with Compliance before launch.

Method and limits

  • Source: public reviews on the Google Play listing, UAE storefront, English only, pulled newest first back to October 2025. Reviewer names are not collected or shown.
  • Classification: keyword rules, multi-label. Rules were tuned on a hand-labelled sample of 59, so their agreement figures are optimistic. A fresh hold-out sample has not been labelled. About 39% of negative reviews match no theme.
  • Language: English only. Hindi, Hinglish and other languages are only partly visible, so the sample under-represents some of the user base.
  • Sampling: not random, and review volume dropped sharply during the year. Month and version cuts are only as good as the count in each cell.
  • Version comparison: compares reviewers by the version they ran. It is an association. Users who stay on v12 after May are a self-selected group, and the decline began before v13 appeared (v12 reviewers were 18% negative in Nov to Feb and 28% in Mar to Apr).
  • Payday test: a two-proportion z-test on share of 1 and 2 star reviews. Review date is when someone complained, not when the failure happened. The split by period was explored after seeing the data, so treat it as exploratory.
  • Mis-taps: about 13% of 1 and 2 star reviews read as positive only, so some low ratings may be accidental.
  • Lifetime rating: the store listing shows 4.3 from 182K ratings. That sits alongside the recent decline in this sample; it doesn't contradict it, because the lifetime figure is dominated by older ratings.
  • Not covered: Apple App Store reviews (39K ratings) are future scope; true failure rates, refund times, support volumes, and anyone who never reviews.
  • Quotes: paraphrased from public reviews, not copied.
